# Step-by-step learning guide

## A practical way to study each project

**Stage 1 — Read the business problem:** explain in one sentence what decision a company wants to make.

**Stage 2 — Inspect the raw CSV:** list what one row represents, identify the target/metric and confirm that units make sense.

**Stage 3 — Walk through the code:** find where the script reads data, creates features, calculates metrics, produces charts and writes outputs.

**Stage 4 — Read the result:** identify the headline number, its units, and whether the result is descriptive, predictive, or statistical.

**Stage 5 — Recommend an action:** distinguish a reasonable next investigation from a conclusion that the example cannot support.

**Stage 6 — Present it:** give a 45-second explanation: “The business wanted to __. I analyzed __ using __. I found __. I recommend __, but a limitation is __.”

## Mini-glossary

- **Churn rate:** customers who left divided by customers studied, for the defined period.
- **ROC AUC:** ability of a classifier's scores to rank positives above negatives.
- **Holdout period:** later records not used to fit the initial forecasting model.
- **MAE:** average of absolute prediction errors, in the same units as the target.
- **Seasonality:** patterns recurring at regular calendar intervals.
- **Rule-based alert:** a record matching stated review conditions; not proof of wrongdoing.
- **Conversion rate:** converters divided by exposed visitors.
- **Percentage points:** difference between two percentages, not a relative percent change.
- **p-value:** a measure of how unusual a test statistic would be under the stated null hypothesis; not the probability the null hypothesis is true.
